## Summary

Mwahahaha! Behold the Concurrency Semaphore, the dimensional regulator that prevents parallel subagent timelines from overwhelming system memory! Left unchecked, an explosion of concurrent workers would exhaust system threads and crash our temporal observation post. Our semaphore bounds active workers to a strictly configured ceiling! The Organization cannot flood our channels!

### Human Decision Point: Option A vs Option B
- **Option A (Active)**: Bounded async semaphore driven by validated `Config.maxConcurrency` in Cordis context.
- **Option B (Alternative)**: Unbounded fire-and-forget worker spawning that exhausts host memory and process limits.

## Behaviour

- **Given** a queue configured with default capacity and 5 pending pieces **When** dispatch is triggered **Then** 4 workers reserve capacity simultaneously while the 5th remains queued and parked.
- **Given** an active worker completes its task and releases its reservation **When** `wake()` evaluates capacity **Then** the queue immediately admits and starts the next eligible pending piece.
- **Given** invalid concurrency values such as 0, 33, or 1.5 **When** the plugin mounts **Then** Schemastery config validation rejects the configuration at load time.

## Teach me while you build

Energy management is paramount in temporal experiments! You cannot open infinite timeline rifts at once!

- **What it does**: Limits how many worker subagents can run simultaneously, queueing the rest until slots free up.
- **Background**: A counting semaphore maintains a set of permits. Threads acquire permits to enter and release permits upon completion.
- **How it works here**: `DevLoopQueue.wake()` counts active reservations via `this.getActiveWorkers().length < this.maxConcurrency`. Eligible queued pieces resolve their startup gate and transition to `'starting'`.
- **Why this approach**: Prevents runaway subagent spawning from depleting host CPU, RAM, and LLM API rate limits.
- **Approaches considered**: Unbounded concurrency (crashes host under load) vs Fixed polling intervals (wastes time) vs Event-driven reactive wakeups (chosen: triggers on lifecycle and completion events).
- **Prior art inspected**: Dijkstra's counting semaphore (1965) and Cordis reactive service lifecycles.
- **What we get for free**: Schemastery schema validation (`z.number().step(1).min(1).max(32).default(4)`) validates configuration before the service starts.
- **From the docs**: AGENTS.md: 'No hardcoded tunables in plugins: deployment-varying choices are validated Config fields changeable from cordis.yml.'
- **What to notice**: Notice that reservations cover startup through complete lease cleanup, not just execution!
- **Concepts to own**: Backpressure: regulating the rate of work intake to match system processing capacity.
- **Interview angle**: How do you design a concurrency-limiting queue in TypeScript? Answer: A reactive capacity check bounding active workers against a validated ceiling with event-driven wakeup.
- **Decisions you can now make alone**: You can safely change `maxConcurrency` in your local `cordis.yml` based on your machine's CPU core count and quota.
- **What you should learn from this**: True power lies in restraint. Bound your concurrency to guarantee world line stability! El Psy Kongroo!
