## Summary

Consider the unexpected anomaly. What if a file appears in `done/` that has no journaled history in durable storage? Or what if a file's local content changes after acceptance? A detective does not sweep anomalies under the rug. We isolate them in an anomalies table and surface them via `/dev-loop-recovery inspect <pieceId>` or acknowledge them via `/dev-loop-recovery acknowledge <anomalyId> <reason...>`. An unresolved anomaly requires human inspection. Acknowledgment records awareness without lifting quarantine or authorizing repair. Fail-closed is the law.

### Human Decision Point: Option A vs Option B
- **Option A (Active)**: Quarantine anomalous pieces in dedicated audit table, reject admissions fail-closed, and require explicit human inspection.
- **Option B (Alternative)**: Silently ignore anomalies or fabricate fake completion records, corrupting the audit trail and violating epistemic bounds.

## Behaviour

- **Given** an unjournaled file discovered in `done/` or an unresolved intent **When** loadLifecycle runs **Then** it records a ReconciliationAnomaly in the anomalies table and flags the piece as quarantined.
- **Given** an operator invoking `/dev-loop-recovery inspect <pieceId>` **When** anomalies exist for that piece **Then** it returns the serialized history and deterministic anomalies list without modifying state.
- **Given** an authorized root human operator invoking `/dev-loop-recovery acknowledge <anomalyId> <reason...>` **When** local observations match the stored anomaly **Then** it durably records the acknowledgment with root command identity while keeping the piece strictly quarantined.

## Teach me while you build

When evidence contradicts theory, quarantine the discrepancy. Never force reality to fit an assumption.

- **What it does**: Detects and isolates irreconcilable state discrepancies (e.g. unjournaled files, changed sources, unresolved intents) in an audit table and allows human inspection.
- **Background**: Automated recovery must have boundaries. If an anomaly cannot be proven mathematically, human intervention is mandatory and fail-closed.
- **How it works here**: Scans local files against durable transition history. Discrepancies generate a `ReconciliationAnomaly` with SHA-256 digests in the `anomalies` table. The `/dev-loop-recovery` command provides `inspect` and `acknowledge` subcommands. Acknowledging records human awareness with command and session IDs, but never clears quarantine or alters lifecycle state.
- **Why this approach**: Prevents phantom files or mutated sources from contaminating the execution pipeline while maintaining an immutable forensic audit log.
- **Approaches considered**: Auto-insert fake completion record (fabricates false history, rejected) vs Delete unjournaled files (risks data loss, rejected) vs Quarantine with root human acknowledgment (chosen: fail-closed and auditable).
- **Prior art inspected**: Forensic evidence chain of custody protocols and dead-letter quarantine (DLQ) in distributed ledgers.
- **What we get for free**: Storage domain persistence ensures anomaly records and human acknowledgments survive process restarts.
- **From the docs**: AGENTS.md: 'Unjournaled historical done files remain explicit anomalies rather than new completion evidence.'
- **What to notice**: Notice that acknowledging an anomaly does NOT clear the quarantine; only genuine human resolution can unblock work!
- **Concepts to own**: Epistemic quarantine: isolating contradictory observations so automated processes fail closed instead of guessing.
- **Interview angle**: How do you handle unresolvable state discrepancies in automated reconciliation engines? Answer: Quarantine discrepancies fail-closed, log forensic observations, and require explicit root human acknowledgment without automatic repair.
- **Decisions you can now make alone**: You know how to add inspection filters for specific anomaly kinds without modifying the durable storage schema.
- **What you should learn from this**: Face contradictions honestly. Quarantine the anomaly, document the forensic evidence, and preserve the paper trail.
