## Summary

Tutturu~ 🌸 Sometimes a piece isn't quite ready, and that is completely okay! We don't want anyone to feel bad or rush into execution. With `/dev-loop reject <id> <reason...>`, the root human developer can defer a `pending` piece and leave helpful notes! The command parser captures the remaining input line literally as a non-empty human reason string, verifies live root authority and piece presence, and transitions the piece from `pending` to `blocked` in `ctx.devLoopLifecycle` with the recorded reason. It gently guides our team so we can improve the piece together later!

### Human Decision Point: Option A vs Option B
- **Option A (Active)**: Capture unquoted trailing remainder as mandatory human rejection reason, verify `pending` status, and transition to `blocked` via lifecycle service.
- **Option B (Alternative)**: Allow empty rejection reasons or arbitrary state jumps (such as `todo`-to-`blocked`), losing constructive feedback and violating lifecycle state invariants.

## Behaviour

- **Given** a piece in `pending` state and a multi-word reason **When** `/dev-loop reject <id> <reason...>` is executed by the root human agent **Then** it transitions the piece from `pending` to `blocked` with the exact literal reason and returns `Piece <id>: blocked.`
- **Given** a reject command without a reason or with only whitespace after the piece ID **When** `parse` runs **Then** it throws a `Refusal` stating `A nonempty reject reason is required.`
- **Given** a piece currently in `todo`, `blocked`, or `done` status **When** reject is executed **Then** it throws a `Refusal` stating that the piece is in that status and reject requires `pending`.
- **Given** an invocation from a child or non-root agent **When** reject is executed **Then** it throws a `Refusal` requiring the exact live root human agent.

## Teach me while you build

Saying 'not yet' is an act of care! Let's make sure feedback is preserved with kindness. Tutturu~

- **What it does**: Allows human operators to reject a piece proposal, marking it `blocked` with a documented reason.
- **Background**: In autonomous agent workflows, human rejection is valuable feedback that guides redesign rather than an error or failure.
- **How it works here**: The command parses the target piece ID and slices the remainder of the line as the reason. It verifies root human authority and checks that the piece exists and is in `pending` status. It then calls `ctx.devLoopLifecycle.transition(id, 'pending', 'blocked', reason, signal)` to record the decision.
- **Why this approach**: Slicing the remainder preserves spaces, punctuation, and notes without requiring complex shell escaping or quotes from the user.
- **Approaches considered**: Immediate deletion of piece files (destructive and loses context) vs Silent unassign (leaves piece in limbo) vs Mandatory reason with `blocked` lifecycle transition (chosen: transparent, constructive, and safe).
- **Prior art inspected**: GitHub PR change request reviews, bug triage states in bugtrackers, and Kanban blocked columns.
- **What we get for free**: `ctx.devLoopLifecycle` persists the transition and rejection reason into durable session logs.
- **From the docs**: plans/AGENTS.md: 'A piece that is superseded rather than completed goes to trash per the override rule, not to done/.'
- **What to notice**: Notice that reject strictly requires `pending` status; an unreviewed `todo` piece cannot jump directly to `blocked` via reject!
- **Concepts to own**: Constructive deferral: capturing actionable intent when gating operations are declined.
- **Interview angle**: How do you design rejection workflows in human-in-the-loop systems? Answer: Require structured rationale, enforce strict state preconditions (`pending` to `blocked`), and record reasons durably.
- **Decisions you can now make alone**: You know how to add operator feedback helpers that inspect blocked pieces and surface human reasons to rework agents.
- **Lesson**: Rejection is just a detour on the road to excellence! Preserve the reason and try again with a smile! 🌸
